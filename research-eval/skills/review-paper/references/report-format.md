# Review output

Adapt this structure to a verified review form when one is available. Keep the recommendation, evidence, confidence, and revisions visible. Omit inapplicable fields and empty headings; do not invent findings to populate a template. Usually a substantive review needs approximately 800–1,500 words, but shorter or longer is appropriate to the draft and user request.

## Recommendation

**Recommendation:** [native label, or fallback label with "simulated" designation]

**Decisive rationale:** Two to four sentences connecting the paper's most important evidence and deficiencies to the applicable bar. Explain a close decision, not merely that it is "borderline."

**Confidence:** High / Moderate / Low, with a specific reason and verification limits.

For journals, add **Editorial recommendation** and **Simulated accept/reject assessment** as separate fields when the editorial scale does not supply the requested accept/reject rating. Explain their relationship. Where a native numeric score is used, state the official score range, direction, and source.

If the verdict is **Insufficient information**, label the entire assessment partial and replace a decisive rationale with the missing material needed. Give only comments the available evidence can support.

## Review context

- Draft identifier/version and materials actually inspected; note unreadable or unavailable items. State whether figures were visually inspected or only captions/text were available.
- Target venue, edition, track/article type, and relevant contribution type; state assumptions.
- Calibration: **verified official criteria**, **user-supplied criteria (unverified)**, or **provisional venue calibration**. For mixed coverage, identify the verified and provisional elements. Link the official sources actually inspected and summarize the decision-relevant rules. Include any unresolved scope/scale details.
- Scope of external verification: venue guidance, specific prior works checked, and whether any code/data/proof checks occurred. Do not imply exhaustive verification.

## Paper summary

A concise, neutral account of the research question, claimed contributions, method, and principal evidence. Identify a material mismatch between the headline claim and the demonstrated result.

## Assessment

Use the official dimension fields when verified; otherwise use a small table of applicable rubric dimensions, qualitative judgments, and concrete reasons. Mark unassessed items explicitly. Do not manufacture numerical subscores.

## Strengths

List substantive strengths with draft locations and their significance. Include only supported strengths, with no fixed count or artificial balance.

## Weaknesses and reviewer comments

For each material concern use this compact structure:

**W1 — [Issue] — [Decision-critical / Substantial / Minor]**

- **Evidence:** Accurate draft location and observation. For omissions, name the sections checked. Explicitly distinguish observation from uncertainty.
- **Why it matters:** Affected claim, criterion, and impact on the recommendation.
- **Requested resolution:** A focused clarification, analysis, experiment, proof repair, or narrower claim. State whether the answer can fit a rebuttal or requires revision/new work.

Group minor comments separately when useful. Correctness, interpretation, and proportionate remedies matter more than a fixed number of comments.

## Questions for the authors

Ask only decision-relevant questions, linked to concern identifiers when helpful. State how the answer could affect the judgment. Do not repeat every weakness as a question or imply that favorable answers are already established.

## Revision priorities

Give a short ordered list: decision-critical repairs first, then high-value improvements, then minor polish. Link each action to the relevant concern. Distinguish **required to support the current claim**, **an alternative narrower claim**, and **optional extension**. Note whether a fix needs clarification, reanalysis, new evidence, or redesign; do not invent time estimates.

End with the evidence that would most plausibly change the recommendation, if any. Keep this conditional; do not promise acceptance after revisions.

# Proposal assessment and calibration

Keep three questions separate: whether a contribution appears new, whether it matters, and whether the planned evidence could make it suitable for a specific venue. No formula or average combines them reliably.

## Proposal recommendation

For a venue that actually evaluates proposals, use its verified native categories and criteria. Otherwise use these **simulated endorsements of the current research plan**, not paper acceptance predictions:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Strong accept | Strongly endorse pursuing the plan: a meaningful contribution is well differentiated within the search, the mechanism and design are credible, and key risks have a concrete way to be tested. This does not assume positive results. |
| Accept | Endorse pursuing it: a useful, plausibly differentiated contribution and workable study are specified, with manageable uncertainties. |
| Weak accept | Tentatively endorse: a credible research path exists, but important bounded issues need clarification or an early pilot before major investment. |
| Weak reject | Revise before substantial investment: promising elements exist, but a consequential gap in differentiation, value, feasibility, or design is currently unresolved. |
| Reject | Do not pursue the present formulation: a central overlap, weak scientific question, unavailable essential resource, or flawed design substantially undermines the plan; a real pivot would be needed. |
| Strong reject | The current plan has a clearly evidenced fundamental failure or central premise already established with no identified meaningful remaining question. Reserve for a well-substantiated case, not unfamiliar terminology or missing final results. |

**Insufficient information** is an abstention when the idea cannot be assessed. **Provisional** qualifies a recommendation when useful plan assessment is possible but literature or venue checks are incomplete. Never label a recommendation unqualified when the decisive novelty question remains unverified. Missing browsing alone does not justify rejecting a direction; it prevents a confident researched novelty claim.

For overlapping work, a recommendation can still be positive if the proposal has a defensible resource, replication, theory, generalization, failure-analysis, or other relevant contribution. Explain which contribution survives and whether the user actually proposed it.

## Claim-level novelty

Use these categories per material contribution, with a cited comparison and confidence:

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Direct precedent found | A verified source already establishes the substantive proposed claim under comparable conditions. State the covered claim precisely. |
| Partial overlap | Prior work covers some components, claims, or settings; assess the importance and support for the remaining difference. |
| Potentially differentiated | The searched literature provides a plausible, substantive distinction, but the search cannot prove absence of prior work. |
| Unresolved | Proposal specificity, access limits, or search coverage prevents a defensible comparison. Name the missing check. |

Do not give an aggregate novelty percentage, infer novelty from low citation counts, or mistake a lack of exact search hits for a new scientific contribution. A full-text direct precedent can support a high-confidence overlap finding even if the broader search is partial; an absence-of-precedent claim needs broader coverage.

## Conditional venue potential

Use **High**, **Plausible**, **Marginal**, **Low**, or **Undetermined**, tied to documented venue criteria. These are qualitative planning judgments, not official categories or calibrated acceptance probabilities.

- **High:** a substantial and well-differentiated contribution plus a credible evidence plan would address the applicable bar if the proposed work succeeds. State the key contingencies.
- **Plausible:** there is a reasonable route, with specific contribution or evidence questions still open.
- **Marginal:** the present expected contribution or evidence plan appears close to or below the bar, with a narrow defensible route that needs explanation.
- **Low:** the current formulation has a substantial scope, contribution, or evidence mismatch.
- **Undetermined:** the venue/track, central contribution, or necessary calibration is unresolved.

If no target is supplied, give a provisional field-level potential assessment rather than claiming venue-specific calibration. Suggest alternative venue types only when they follow from the contribution; named venue recommendations need inspected official scope. A workshop is not automatically appropriate or easy, and a journal does not universally require a larger number of experiments.

Separate **eligible submission type now** from **potential of a future completed paper**. No finished experiments is normal for a proposal and can still mean it is not currently eligible as a full research paper. If considering accepted papers for calibration, inspect comparable contribution types and do not treat observed patterns as official requirements or a representative acceptance sample.

## Defensible nuance and contribution

Replace a vague claim such as "apply method A to domain B" with a precise question only if warranted by the proposal and sources:

> Under [specified conditions], does [mechanism/intervention] address [specific failure or uncertainty] beyond [closest existing approach], as distinguished by [decisive test]?

This is a scaffold, not a requirement for all disciplines. A resource, qualitative account, theoretical result, or replication may need a different formulation.

Test whether the distinction is:

- **Substantive:** changes the question, knowledge, assumptions, capability, evidence, or practical constraint in a consequential way.
- **Verifiable:** has an appropriate proof, analysis, observation, or comparison that could support or refute it.
- **Not already established in the sources checked:** cite the closest competitor and bound this conclusion by access/coverage.

A new label, extra benchmark, small accuracy difference, or component combination is not inherently sufficient or inherently worthless. Explain the consequence. Distinguish "known method, new scientifically informative finding" from "new method" and avoid inflated novelty claims.

## Reviewer comments and uncertainty

Each substantive concern should identify proposal evidence/location or missing detail, supporting literature when relevant, consequence, and a proportionate remedy. Label **Decision-critical**, **Substantial**, or **Minor**. Avoid a fixed criticism quota. State the strongest likely reviewer objection and the evidence that would answer it, not an invented committee consensus.

Report **High / Moderate / Low** confidence separately for the literature comparison and the proposal/venue assessment. Explain access and specialization limits. Do not imply proofs, analyses, data availability, or implementation details were verified when only described by the proposer. No bias-free or acceptance guarantee.

# Proposal review report

Adapt length to the complexity of the proposal and research performed. Keep the decision and strongest evidence visible, with no artificial citation or comment quota. A researched report will often need 1,200–2,000 words plus the search log; honor a requested shorter form by compressing prose rather than hiding uncertainty.

## Verdict

- **Proposal recommendation:** Native proposal-review label if verified; otherwise simulated Strong accept / Accept / Weak accept / Weak reject / Reject / Strong reject. Explicitly label this as endorsement of a research plan, not future paper acceptance.
- **Novelty:** Claim-specific verdicts: Direct precedent found / Partial overlap / Potentially differentiated / Unresolved. Identify the closest competitor or missing comparison.
- **Venue potential:** High / Plausible / Marginal / Low / Undetermined, conditional on the particular evidence still needed. State whether this concerns a future paper or an actual proposal submission.
- **Research status:** Performed / Partial / Not performed, with search date or historical cutoff and the most material gap. List verification status for venue criteria separately.
- **Confidence:** Literature comparison and proposal/venue assessment, each with a reason. Make provisional status visible when research is incomplete.

Explain the verdict in a short paragraph. State what is already established, what meaningful question could remain, and what most strongly limits the present plan. For Insufficient information, give the assessment that is possible and request the missing material without an invented rating.

## What the proposal actually claims

Restate the question, hypothesis, mechanism/approach, contribution claims, proposed setting, and evidence plan neutrally. Distinguish supplied facts, preliminary findings, planned results, and your assumptions. Identify the target venue/year/track and submission type, or state that calibration is provisional.

## Closest related work: has it been done?

Use a comparison table:

| Work and primary-source link | What it establishes; source location/access level | Overlap with a specific proposal claim | Meaningful remaining difference or unresolved check | Consequence for novelty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rows should cover the closest competitors and any foundational work needed to understand the distinction. Include verified dates/publication status in the work label or source list. Separate preprint and published versions only if their differences matter. Be precise about which claims have direct precedent; do not blanket-label the proposal duplicate based on a component alone.

After the table, provide claim-level judgments and a short synthesis. Which headline claims should be dropped, narrowed, or retained? Which comparison could most plausibly overturn the novelty judgment? Mark claims based on abstract-only sources as limited; avoid definitive claims about uninspected details.

## Scientific value, feasibility, and venue fit

Assess the relevant dimensions with reasons rather than invented numeric scores. Cite the inspected official venue guidance and distinguish its requirements from your judgment. State whether the work has a plausible path to the bar and which findings would be needed. Missing final results is a future dependency, not a demonstrated methodological failure.

## Reviewer comments

Give supported strengths and prioritized concerns. Each concern should include severity, proposal location or missing detail, relevant source, why it matters, and a proportionate resolution. Include the strongest acceptance case and strongest skeptical objection without pretending to be separate reviewers. Ask only questions whose answers could change the assessment.

## The nuance and strongest defensible contribution

Explain the scientific distinction that matters for this particular idea. Offer a concise contribution statement with bounded claims. Label each recommendation as **clarify existing plan**, **new work required**, or **pivot**. Ground new directions in the comparison table, or mark their novelty unchecked and avoid endorsing it. A cosmetic rewrite does not cure a scientific overlap.

When useful, contrast an overbroad proposed claim with a more defensible formulation. Do not claim results that have not been obtained. If there is no credible differentiated direction within the evidence checked, say so instead of inventing one.

## Minimum decisive study and revision priorities

Specify the core hypothesis/question, closest appropriate baseline or counterhypothesis, critical control/design choice, outcome that would support or undermine the contribution, and next go/no-go decision. Adapt this to theory, qualitative work, resources, or replication. Surface data-access/resource dependencies before ambitious experiment lists.

Give ordered actions, separating necessary changes from optional extensions. State a useful null/negative outcome or fallback where supported. No invented effect sizes, sample sizes, compute estimates, or promises of acceptance.

## Sources and search coverage

Include the primary sources actually inspected, with verified metadata and stable links; cross-reference them from the comparison. Add the compact actual search log (query, retrieval source, date, useful results and inspected sources), citation-chasing performed, and inaccessible/unsearched branches. State the stopping reason. Do not disguise a bounded search as an exhaustive systematic review or absence of precedent as proof of novelty.
